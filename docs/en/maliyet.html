The town and open country rows use the same board: the amplified SX1280 module E28-2G4M20S, an outdoor 5 dBi whip, the same microcontroller and secure element. In the tunnel the board carries the DWM3000 UWB module. What differs is not the board but the structure it goes on: a lighting column with mains in town, a distribution pole with no mains and a solar panel in open country, a bracket that waits on a lane closure in the tunnel.

On a raised mast the build costs 52 times what it does on a lighting column. The same unit on the same ground; the only difference is what it is fitted to.

A unit is cheapest to build and run in town, yet the town row is the dearest per square kilometre. The reason is density: buildings cut the signal, so a unit serves 0,43 km² in town and 6,81 km² in open country. 19 units over 8,14 km² are 2,33 units a km²; 52 units over 354,00 km² are 0,15. The cost per square kilometre is the cost per unit times that density.

Power is only one of the two things a structure gives. A signalised junction carries a controller cabinet: mains for the heads and a line to the traffic management centre. The municipality's cameras already use that line. Some of the units stand at such a junction and reach the centre through it. No unit carries a SIM card: the rest are watched by the phones and vehicle receivers that range against them, and a unit that stops answering shows up that way.

Adding anchors raises availability, but not for nothing. What the existing structures do is make that trade affordable, because each extra unit is a board and a fitting rather than a site.

Where the prices came from

  • The report gives only two totals per product and no part prices. Here each main part carries its own distributor price, and so does the rest of the board: mains supply, regulator, protection, terminals, antenna cable, box, passives, the printed board and its assembly. What is left of the report's total once the main parts are taken out, its "other" line, stands beside them for comparison and is not added in.
  • A part whose distributor tier was verified enters at that price at every quantity from its tier up. Below it, that price is a floor: fewer of a part never cost less than more of it. Parts with no verified tier and the "other" line use the report's own discount from one to a hundred, and 90 % of the hundred price at a thousand. So for every product one unit costs more than a hundred, and a hundred more than a thousand. Because the discount comes from each product's own report line, the same part with no verified tier (the box, the printed board) takes a slightly different thousand price in each product. The cost table uses the thousand.
  • The part prices are the sellers' list prices of 23 to 28 September 2026; the links are above. They are to be confirmed by quotation before ordering. If a price changes, one line in bom.toml changes and this page and the table follow.

What every figure rests on

The crew and its day

  1. One crew day: 17570 TL/day (worked out)

    A day of the fitting and maintenance crew: bucket truck 2000 TL x 8 hours x 0,7 = 11200 TL (a certified operator comes with the hire); electrician, a thirtieth of the middle of the monthly range, 95000 TL, 3170 TL; helper, the large-city top of the band, 3200 TL. 17570 TL in all. A maintenance visit is this divided by the sites the crew reaches on that structure in a day: 17570 / 8 = 2196 TL on a lighting column, 17570 / 4 = 4392 TL on a rural distribution pole and in a tunnel. A roof needs no bucket truck: 6370 / 4 = 1592 TL.

    Source: bucket truck: a 20 m bucket truck is about 2000 TL an hour in Ankara, 30 % off by the day (Ankara Sepetli Vinç Kiralama, price guide, 2026); labour: an electrician 80000-110000 TL a month, a labourer 2500-3200 TL a day, near the top in large cities (Yapı Arenası, 12 August 2026)

  2. Maintenance crew: 2 people (worked out)

    How many people are paid a per diem: the electrician and helper priced into the crew day. The bucket truck's operator comes with the hire, and the hire company pays his per diem.

    Source: the crew in the crew day: an electrician and a helper (Yapı Arenası, 12 August 2026); the bucket truck's operator belongs to the hire company

  3. A crew day on roofs: 6370 TL (worked out)

    A crew day on roofs: no bucket truck, the roof is reached by the stairs. Electrician, a thirtieth of the middle of the monthly range, 95000 TL, 3170 TL; helper, the large-city top of the band, 3200 TL. 6370 TL in all.

    Source: labour: an electrician 80000-110000 TL a month, a labourer 2500-3200 TL a day, near the top in large cities (Yapı Arenası, 12 August 2026)

Sites a crew covers in a day

  1. Lighting columns: 8 sites/day (worked out)

    How many lighting columns a crew fits or visits in a day. A published example: 11 crews replaced 414 luminaires on 207 poles over one weekend, 18,8 poles and 37,6 luminaires a crew. The report does not give the hours, only that the work ran day and night; counting 12 hours of each of the two days, a crew replaced 1,6 luminaires an hour, 12,5 in an eight-hour day. A YERKON unit is one clamp and one supply tap on a column, no more work than a luminaire. 8 a day is a cautious figure below that; an hour a column.

    Source: Dicle Elektrik, Diyarbakır Suriçi, 25 June 2026: 11 crews with 11 bucket trucks replaced 414 luminaires on 207 poles over one weekend

  2. Rural distribution poles: 4 sites/day (worked out)

    How many distribution poles a crew reaches in a day in open country. Town counts an hour of work a column (columns a crew reaches in a day, the Dicle Elektrik example). Open country adds the road: two hours of the eight-hour day to and from the base, half an hour between sites (this project's estimate of the road). (8 - 2) / (1 + 0,5) = 4.

    Source: Dicle Elektrik, Diyarbakır Suriçi, 25 June 2026: 11 crews with 11 bucket trucks replaced 414 luminaires on 207 poles over one weekend

  3. Tunnel: 4 sites/day (worked out)

    How many units a crew fits or visits in a tunnel in a day. The work waits on the highways authority's lane closure, whose setting up and taking down takes part of the day. The work per unit is about an hour as in town (the Dicle Elektrik example); the time lost to the closure is counted like the road in open country: 4 a day.

    Source: Dicle Elektrik, Diyarbakır Suriçi, 25 June 2026: 11 crews with 11 bucket trucks replaced 414 luminaires on 207 poles over one weekend

  4. Roofs: 4 sites/day (worked out)

    How many roofs a crew reaches in a day. The work on a roof is as much as on a column (about an hour, the Dicle Elektrik example), but each building needs its manager found for the permission and the key; that wait is counted like the road: 4 a day.

    Source: Dicle Elektrik, Diyarbakır Suriçi, 25 June 2026: 11 crews with 11 bucket trucks replaced 414 luminaires on 207 poles over one weekend

  5. Raised masts: 4 sites/day (worked out)

    How many 25 m masts a crew reaches in a day. A 20 m bucket truck falls short, so the mast is climbed; masts stand far apart. The same figure as a rural distribution pole.

    Source: Dicle Elektrik, Diyarbakır Suriçi, 25 June 2026: 11 crews with 11 bucket trucks replaced 414 luminaires on 207 poles over one weekend

How high the unit stands

  1. On a lighting column: 12 m (standard)

    How tall the lighting column a unit stands on is. In TEDAŞ's table the heights are a set, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14 m, by road class. Kızılay's main streets are M1 to M3, so 10 to 14 m; 12 m is the middle of that. The town row was run at every value of the set: at 8 m HPE P95 9,19 m, availability 94,94 %, CAPEX 10010 TL/km²; at 10 m 9,04 m, 96,30 %, 9296 TL/km²; at 12 m the table's own figures; at 14 m 8,74 m, 96,82 %, 8916 TL/km².

    Source: TEDAŞ, rules for LED road lighting design (August 2022), annex 1: columns 12 to 14 m on M1 roads, 10 to 12 m on M2 and M3, 8 to 10 m on M4, 8 m on M5, 6 to 8 m on footways

  2. On a distribution pole: 10 m (assumption)

    How high a unit stands on a rural distribution pole: the pole's height above ground less 2,5 m to the medium-voltage conductors. The pole lengths are a set (TEDAŞ) and the distance comes from the regulation; no source was found for how much of the pole is buried, so the value is still an assumption. The rural row was run at three heights on the same placement: at 6 m HPE P95 8,58 m, availability 91,11 %, area 327,50 km², CAPEX 1216 TL/km²; at 8 m 8,15 m, 93,17 %, 344,58 km², 1156 TL/km²; at 10 m the table's own figures. The lower the unit, the less it covers and the less often it gives a position.

    Source: pole lengths: TEDAŞ-MLZ/99-34, spun concrete pole specification (9,3-10-11 m, 12-13-14 m, 15-16 m and up); distance from the conductors: the Regulation on Electrical Power Installations, least vertical distance from overhead conductors to communication lines 2,5 m at medium voltage and 1 m at low voltage; no source was found for the buried depth

  3. On a roof: 2 m (assumption)

    How far the roof bracket lifts the unit above the roof. The unit's height above the ground is the building's own height plus this; the building's height is read from the fetched footprints, one building at a time.

    Source: this project

  4. On a raised mast: 25 m (assumption)

    Purpose-built mast. 25 m is the height a 10 km link needs against a 2 m vehicle antenna over flat ground.

    Source: this project

Power

  1. One unit's yearly consumption: 3 kWh/year (worked out)

    One broadcast unit's yearly consumption. The unit spends nearly all its time listening: the SX1280 draws a few mA doing that and the microcontroller one or two, under 30 mW together at 3,3 V. With the mains converter's idle loss and the replies it sends, the average stays under 0,34 W, which is 3 kWh a year. That is a generous upper bound.

    Source: SX1280 and STM32G0 datasheets, this project's arithmetic

  2. Electricity tariff: 5,62 TL/kWh (worked out)

    What a unit's electricity costs a kilowatt-hour. A unit cannot draw from a lighting column's public lighting circuit for free: the General Lighting Regulation allows no energy from that connection for any purpose other than public lighting; the unit counts as a separately metered commercial subscriber. 4,78 x 1,175 = 5,62 TL/kWh.

    Source: commercial, low voltage, single rate, taxes included 4,78 TL/kWh, January 2026 (forelektrik.com, 26 December 2025); the increase from 4 April 2026, low-voltage public and private services 17,5 % (Milliyet Uzmanpara, 4 April 2026)

  3. Solar panel and battery: 2090 TL (worked out)

    Panel, battery, controller, bracket, box and cable for a unit on a structure with no mains. A 0,34 W load needs 8 Wh a day: a 10 W panel gives that on two hours of full sun in an Ankara winter, and an 84 Wh battery carries five days. Panel 518,72 + battery 524,40 + controller 276,67 + bracket 421,01 + an IP65 box for the battery and controller 147,41 + 5 m of cable at the top price of 40 TL a metre, 200 = 2088,21, rounded to 2090 TL.

    Source: Akakçe, September 2026, the cheapest found: 10 W solar panel 518,72 TL; 12 V 7 Ah sealed battery 524,40 TL; 10 A PWM charge controller 276,67 TL; ABS solar panel mounting bracket set 421,01 TL; Çet-San thermoplastic surface box 260x180x75, IP65, 147,41 TL; 4 mm² solar cable 25-40 TL a metre (Power Enerji, solar cable prices 2026)

Maintenance and per diem

  1. Maintenance visits a year: 0,20 visits/year (design choice)

    How often an anchor is visited a year. The central system sees every unit's status (the report: the units' records are monitored), so a crew goes when one fails rather than by the calendar. One failure in five years is a pessimistic figure for a sealed board.

    Source: this project, as an operating choice

  2. Extra off-grid visits: 0,20 visits/year (worked out)

    The extra attendance an off-grid site needs: the battery is replaced every five years (the battery's life), so 1 / 5 = 0,2 visits a year; the panel is cleaned on that visit.

    Source: Turkish Revenue Administration depreciation list, 3.14.7: accumulators, 5 years, 20 %

  3. Per diem, a person a day: 850 TL/day (standard)

    The daily allowance of one crew member for a day out of the city. In the private sector the tax-exempt part follows Income Tax Law 24/2: the allowance of a civil servant on the same salary level; grades 5-15 (850 TL) are taken as the nearest level for a field technician. Whether a same-day trip earns all of it or part is not verified; all of it is used, the cautious side for cost. No lodging: both sites are a day trip.

    Source: Law 7567, 2026 Central Government Budget, Schedule H, B-e: grades 5-15, domestic daily allowance 850 TL

  4. Share of it on a day trip: 0,33 fraction (standard)

    A maintenance visit is a day trip: the crew leaves in the morning and is back in the afternoon, so it spends the lunch time away: a third of the allowance (283,33 TL a person). Past 19.00 it would be two thirds.

    Source: Travel Allowance Law 6245, Article 39: a third of the daily allowance for one of the lunch (13.00) or dinner (19.00) times away, two thirds for both, all of it for a night

  5. Does the town crew leave its duty station: no (design choice)

    No. Travel Allowance Law, Article 3(g): in a metropolitan province the place of duty is the district the worker belongs to and the built-up area that continues it. A crew based in Çankaya serves Kızılay; no allowance inside the place of duty (Article 39).

    Source: this project, the maintenance crew is based in central Ankara

  6. Does the open-country crew leave its duty station: no (design choice)

    No: a local technical firm in Polatlı does the maintenance, no crew travels from Ankara and there is no per diem. It does not rest on the distribution company's crews, because the company may not want the work. The poles are still the company's: climbing one may need its permission and conditions, to be settled in the public protocol.

    Source: maintenance model: a local technical firm

  7. Does the tunnel crew leave its duty station: no (design choice)

    No: a local technical firm in Kızılcahamam does the maintenance, no per diem. Entering the bore and closing a lane still need the highways authority's permission; visits can be timed to its maintenance closures.

    Source: maintenance model: a local technical firm

Rent and data

  1. Distribution pole rent, a year: 0 TL/year (design choice)

    What the distribution company charges a year to carry a unit on its pole: nothing. The units go up under a public-sector agreement, with the prospect of being included in public structures that already stand, such as ITS cabinets, roadside units and distribution poles. Nor has any distribution company published a fee for equipment on its poles: the transport ministry's rights-of-way rules, Türk Telekom's connection fees and the General Lighting Regulation name no per-pole fee. Fees charged to commercial users, for comparison: distribution companies have rented advertising space on their poles since 2015 with the regulator's leave, the smallest poster 4700 TL a month (Tr724, 11 May 2019); Istanbul Metropolitan Municipality's 2025 tariff charges 24500 TL a year for a repeater or small cell on its property, and twice the site selection fee for a cabinet on a BEDAŞ or AYEDAŞ lighting column. Without the agreement, 1200 TL a pole a year would raise rural OPEX by about half.

    Source: this project, as an operating choice: a public-sector agreement

  2. Roof rent, a year: 1200 TL/year (assumption)

    What the building's owner is paid a year for the roof. No published figure was found; 100 TL a month is assumed, the sum used for a distribution pole as a comparison should there be no protocol. Base station roof rents are far above this, but those are for cabinets drawing kilowatts; this unit fits in a palm. Roofs are rented, so the placement search does not offer them; this figure touches no row of the table.

    Source: this project

  3. Cellular data line, a year: 0 TL/year (design choice)

    What a year of data costs an anchor with no line of its own: nothing, because no unit carries a SIM card. The tie to the centre runs through the tunnel's own communications spine in a tunnel and a signalised junction's cabinet in town. The other units are watched by the phones and vehicle receivers that range against them: those already know which unit answered, so a unit that stops answering shows up without a line of its own.

    Source: this project, as a design choice

Service life and the central system

  1. A unit's service life: 10 years (standard)

    How many years a unit lasts before it is replaced. An anchor is a radio system, and the tax rules give that 10 years. The positioning line (3.49.8, 5 years) is for devices that locate by satellite, which an anchor does not.

    Source: Turkish Revenue Administration depreciation list, 3.49.4: radio telephone, telegraph or broadcast receiving devices and systems, 10 years, 10 %

  2. The battery's life: 5 years (standard)

    The battery's useful life.

    Source: Turkish Revenue Administration depreciation list, 3.14.7: accumulators, 5 years, 20 %

  3. Central system, a year: 237000 TL/year (worked out)

    Running the central management system for a year: the software that watches the units' records and tells the crew of a failure, and the person who runs it. One day a week of a developer: 95000 x 12 x 0,2 = 228000 TL; two servers backing each other up: 2 x 374,90 x 12 = 8998 TL. 236998, rounded to 237000 TL. One day a week is this project's operating choice.

    Source: median developer salary, companies in Türkiye, net 95000 TL a month (Yazılımcı Maaşları 2026 survey, February 2026, 1223 respondents); a 6 vCPU, 8 GB virtual server 374,90 TL a month (Karekod, cheapest VDS prices, 25 September 2026)

  4. Units sharing the centre: 1000 units (design choice)

    How many units the central system is spread over. One person one day a week and two servers can watch the records of a thousand units; a national network spreads it wider still. That is why this is a rate a unit and not a constant added to every deployment: 237000 / 1000 = 237 TL a year.

    Source: this project, as an operating choice
