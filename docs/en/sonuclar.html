What the columns mean

ColumnMeaning
HPE, VPEHorizontal and vertical position error in metres, at the stated percentile, over the journeys evaluated.
AvailabilityThe share of attempted fixes that produced a valid position. It counts modelled failures only and is not a service availability figure.
AreaThe ground where enough units are reachable for a position, swept over the real terrain. It is not the ground a packet reaches.
CAPEXUnit hardware cost divided by the service area. Main hardware only.
OPEXYearly operating cost per km². The report leaves this column empty; this one comes from an itemised inventory.

Three things the table will not do quietly

  • The tunnel's cost per km² does not compare to the other rows. Twelve metres wide over two kilometres is a fiftieth of a square kilometre, so dividing by it produces a large number by arithmetic rather than by judgement. A tunnel serves a line, so compare it on cost per route kilometre.
  • The service area is where a position can be had, not where a packet arrives. In open country a packet reaches 2,6 times the ground a position can be had on.
  • The percentages are not the same metric. The availability the satellite providers publish rests on different test definitions, durations and thresholds. It should not be read as the result of one protocol.

The fast reading

The fast button in the simulation and --fast on the command line coarsen the same two figures: the shadows are drawn once instead of pooled over eight, and the ground profile is read at a fixed 64 samples instead of every 10 m. A run drops from fifteen minutes to about one.

Both coarsenings read the loss low, so a fast answer flatters the deployment: the rural row's P95 came out 4,13 m optimistic. A row that looks bad under a fast reading is genuinely bad, and this page shows the published run only.

YERKON is a project submitted to the UDHAM idea competition of the Ministry of Transport and Infrastructure. The numbers in the table come from the simulation's published run, and what it does not model is written on the simulation page.
