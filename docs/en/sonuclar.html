What the columns mean

ColumnMeaning
HPE, VPEHorizontal and vertical position error in metres, at the stated percentile, over the journeys evaluated.
AvailabilityThe share of attempted fixes that produced a valid position. It counts modelled failures only.
AreaThe ground where enough anchors are reachable for a position, swept over the real terrain. It is not the ground a packet reaches.
CAPEXAnchor hardware cost divided by the service area. Hardware only.
OPEXYearly operating cost per km². The report leaves this column empty; this one comes from an itemised inventory (ADR-0006).

Three things the table will not do quietly

  • The tunnel's cost per km² does not compare to the other rows. Twelve metres wide over two kilometres is a fiftieth of a square kilometre, so dividing by it produces a large number by arithmetic rather than by judgement. A tunnel serves a line, so compare it on cost per route kilometre, which yerkon table prints beside the row.
  • The service area is where a position can be had, not where a packet arrives. In open country a packet reaches 2,6 times the ground a position can be had on, and the notes print both every time (ADR-0012).
  • Terrain and the length of a round decide rural availability. Not one rural link fails on distance; every failure would close with the ground taken away. More anchors is the wrong instinct (ADR-0022).

The fast reading

The fast button in the simulator and --fast on the command line coarsen the same two figures: the shadows are drawn once instead of pooled over eight, and the profile is read at a fixed 64 samples instead of every 10 m. A run drops from fifteen minutes to about one.

Both coarsenings read the loss low, so a fast answer flatters the deployment: the rural row's P95 came out 4,13 m optimistic. A row that looks bad under a fast reading is genuinely bad, and this page shows the published run only (ADR-0063).
