YERKON

YERKON is a proposal for road transport to depend less on foreign satellites for position. A cheap broadcast unit goes onto masts and cabinets already standing by the road, and the receiver in a vehicle finds where it is by measuring its distance to the units around it.

The full application report, in Turkish: YERKON report (PDF, 20 pages)

What it proposes

The Ministry of Transport and Infrastructure already has points on the power and communications network: intelligent transport cabinets, roadside units, traffic lights, tunnel lighting and toll gates. YERKON adds a low cost radio broadcast unit to those points. The unit broadcasts its identity and the position surveyed when it was installed. Where those points are sparse, the other poles along the road are used: in the simulation, lighting columns in town and mostly electricity distribution poles in open country.

A broadcast unit costs roughly 1.400 lira at a thousand units, box, printed board and assembly included. The aim is to reuse the power and communications already standing at intelligent transport points, and that is what keeps the cost down.

YERKON does not aim to replace the satellites. While they work, the two positions sit side by side, and comparing them can help detect a spoofing attack.

The problemNearly everything that finds a position or gives directions today runs on GPS, Galileo, GLONASS and BeiDou. The dependency does not stop at vehicle navigation: emergency crews, public fleets, unmanned vehicles and freight all hang on the same four systems.The systemThree parts: broadcast units standing at surveyed positions, receivers that work out their own position, and a management system that keeps the identities current.ResearchThe project is looking for answers to four questions. None of them is closed, and under each one is what will be done.What it is forSo that transport and the services that need a position do not stop where position is vital and the satellite signal is cut, weakened or no longer trusted; how it is commercialised closes the page.ResultsThirteen systems side by side under the same columns.
