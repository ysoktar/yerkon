The town and open country rows use the same broadcast unit: the amplified SX1280 module E28-2G4M20S, an outdoor 5 dBi whip, the same microcontroller and secure element. In the tunnel the unit carries the DWM3000 UWB module. What differs is not the unit but the structure it goes on: a lighting column with mains in town, a distribution pole with no mains and a solar panel in open country, a bracket that waits on a lane closure in the tunnel.

On a raised mast the build costs 50 times what it does on a lighting column. The same unit on the same ground; the only difference is what it is fitted to.

Of the structures the table uses, a unit is cheapest to build and run on a town lighting column, yet the town row is the dearest per square kilometre. The reason is density: buildings cut the signal, so a unit serves 0,43 km² in town and 6,81 km² in open country. 19 units over 8,14 km² are 2,33 units a km²; 52 units over 354,00 km² are 0,15. The cost per square kilometre is the cost per unit times that density.

A structure gives two things: power and a line to the centre. A signalised junction carries a controller cabinet with both the mains and a line to the traffic management centre, which the municipality's cameras already use. A unit on a structure with a line reaches the centre over it: a signalised junction in town, the tunnel's communications spine in a tunnel, an ITS point on the road in open country. A unit with no line, such as one on a distribution pole far from an ITS point, keeps running on its solar panel and passes its messages by its own radio to the nearest unit that has a line; no unit carries a SIM card. Each unit sends the centre a short "still working" message at regular intervals, and a unit whose message stops coming is taken as failed and a crew is sent. When a receiver sees GPS jamming or spoofing it passes that to a nearby broadcast unit, and the warning reaches the centre the same way.

Adding anchors raises availability, but not for nothing. What the existing structures do is make that trade affordable, because each extra unit is a device and a fitting rather than a site.

Where the prices came from

  • Every part carries its seller's own price ladder: the main parts and the rest of the unit, that is the mains supply, regulator, protection, terminals, antenna cable, box, passives, the printed board and its assembly. Where a unit holds more than one of a part (terminals, passives), the tier is the one the pieces for that many units reach.
  • Where a seller published no tier for a quantity, no discount is assumed and the last known tier holds. The boxes are priced on request above a hundred, and no volume price could be read for the printed board or the battery; those enter at their one-unit price at every quantity. Assembly setup, stencil and part loading fees are paid once an order, so each unit's share shrinks as the order grows. The cost table uses the thousand.
  • The part prices are the sellers' list prices of 25 to 28 September 2026; the links are above. They are to be confirmed by quotation before ordering. If a price changes, this page and the table follow on their own.

The crew and its day

  1. One crew day: 17.570 TL/day (worked out)

    A day of the fitting and maintenance crew: bucket truck 2.000 TL x 8 hours x 0,7 = 11.200 TL (a certified operator comes with the hire); electrician, a thirtieth of the middle of the monthly range, 95.000 TL, 3.170 TL; helper, the large-city top of the band, 3.200 TL. 17.570 TL in all. A maintenance visit is this divided by the sites the crew reaches on that structure in a day: 17.570 / 8 = 2.196 TL on a lighting column, 17.570 / 4 = 4.392 TL on a rural distribution pole and in a tunnel. A roof needs no bucket truck: 6.370 / 4 = 1.592 TL.

Sites a crew covers in a day

  1. Lighting columns: 8 sites/day (worked out)

    How many lighting columns a crew fits or visits in a day. A published example: 11 crews replaced 414 luminaires on 207 poles over one weekend, 18,8 poles and 37,6 luminaires a crew. The report does not give the hours, only that the work ran day and night; counting 12 hours of each of the two days, a crew replaced 1,6 luminaires an hour, 12,5 in an eight-hour day. A YERKON unit is one clamp and one supply tap on a column, no more work than a luminaire. 8 a day is a cautious figure below that; an hour a column.

How high the unit stands

  1. On a lighting column: 12 m (standard)

    How tall the lighting column a unit stands on is. In TEDAŞ's table the heights are a set, 6, 8, 10, 12, 14 m, by road class. Kızılay's main streets are M1 to M3, so 10 to 14 m; 12 m is the middle of that. The town row was run at every value of the set (29 September 2026): at 8 m HPE P95 9,07 m, availability 94,96 %, CAPEX 10053 TL/km²; at 10 m 8,83 m, 96,18 %, 9.336 TL/km²; at 12 m the table's own figures; at 14 m 8,67 m, 96,82 %, 8.954 TL/km². Availability and cost improve smoothly with height and P95 does not: P95 at 12 m is higher than at 10 m and 14 m. P95 is the tail of eight shadow draws, so it moves this much.

    Source: TEDAŞ, rules for LED road lighting design (August 2022), annex 1: columns 12 to 14 m on M1 roads, 10 to 12 m on M2 and M3, 8 to 10 m on M4, 8 m on M5, 6 to 8 m on footways

  2. On a distribution pole: 10 m (assumption)

  3. On a roof: 2 m (assumption)

    How far the roof bracket lifts the unit above the roof. The unit's height above the ground is the building's own height plus this; the building's height is read from the fetched footprints, one building at a time.

Power

  1. One unit's yearly consumption: 3 kWh/year (worked out)

    One broadcast unit's yearly consumption. The unit spends nearly all its time listening: the SX1280 draws a few mA doing that and the microcontroller one or two, under 30 mW together at 3,3 V. With the mains converter's idle loss and the replies it sends, the average stays under 0,34 W, which is 3 kWh a year. That is a generous upper bound.

Maintenance and per diem

  1. Maintenance visits a year: 0,20 visits/year (design choice)

    How often an anchor is visited a year. The central system sees every unit's status (each unit sends a regular "still working" message), so a crew goes when one fails rather than by the calendar. One failure in five years is a pessimistic figure for a sealed board.

    Source: YERKON design choice

  2. Extra off-grid visits: 0,20 visits/year (worked out)

    The extra attendance an off-grid site needs: the battery is replaced every five years (the battery's life), so 1 / 5 = 0,2 visits a year; the panel is cleaned on that visit.

    Source: Turkish Revenue Administration depreciation list, 3.14.7: accumulators, 5 years, 20 %

    Revenue Administration: depreciation rates table (3.49.4 radio equipment and systems 10 years, 3.14.7 accumulators 5 years)

  3. Per diem, a person a day: 850 TL/day (standard)

    The daily allowance of one crew member for a day out of the city. In the private sector the tax-exempt part follows Income Tax Law 24/2: the allowance of a civil servant on the same salary level; grades 5-15 (850 TL) are taken as the nearest level for a field technician. Whether a same-day trip earns all of it or part is not verified; all of it is used, the cautious side for cost. No lodging: both sites are a day trip.

    Source: Law 7567, 2026 Central Government Budget, Schedule H, B-e: grades 5-15, domestic daily allowance 850 TL

    Travel Allowance Law 6245: place of duty (Article 3(g)), allowance on a day trip (Article 39) · Income Tax Law 193, Article 24(2): the tax-exempt part of daily allowances · Presidency of Strategy and Budget: 2026 Central Government Budget Law No. 7567, Schedule H (domestic daily allowances; grades 5-15, 850 TL)

  4. Share of it on a day trip: 0,33 fraction (standard)

    A maintenance visit is a day trip: the crew leaves in the morning and is back in the afternoon, so it spends the lunch time away: a third of the allowance (283,33 TL a person). Past 19.00 it would be two thirds.

    Source: Travel Allowance Law 6245, Article 39: a third of the daily allowance for one of the lunch (13.00) or dinner (19.00) times away, two thirds for both, all of it for a night

    Travel Allowance Law 6245: place of duty (Article 3(g)), allowance on a day trip (Article 39)

  5. Does the town crew leave its duty station: no (design choice)

    No. Travel Allowance Law, Article 3(g): in a metropolitan province the place of duty is the district the worker belongs to and the built-up area that continues it. A crew based in Çankaya serves Kızılay; no allowance inside the place of duty (Article 39).

    Source: YERKON design choice, the maintenance crew is based in central Ankara

    Travel Allowance Law 6245: place of duty (Article 3(g)), allowance on a day trip (Article 39)

  6. Does the open-country crew leave its duty station: no (design choice)

    No: a local technical firm in Polatlı does the maintenance, no crew travels from Ankara and there is no per diem. It does not rest on the distribution company's crews, because the company may not want the work. The poles are still the company's: climbing one may need its permission and conditions, to be settled in the public protocol.

    Source: maintenance model: a local technical firm

  7. Does the tunnel crew leave its duty station: no (design choice)

    No: a local technical firm in Kızılcahamam does the maintenance, no per diem. Entering the bore and closing a lane still need the highways authority's permission; visits can be timed to its maintenance closures.

Rent and data

  1. Distribution pole rent, a year: 0 TL/year (design choice)

    What the distribution company charges a year to carry a unit on its pole: nothing. The units go up under a public-sector agreement, with the prospect of being included in public structures that already stand, such as ITS cabinets, roadside units and distribution poles. Nor has any distribution company published a fee for equipment on its poles: the transport ministry's rights-of-way rules, Türk Telekom's connection fees and the General Lighting Regulation name no per-pole fee. Fees charged to commercial users, for comparison: distribution companies have rented advertising space on their poles since 2015 with the regulator's leave, the smallest poster 4.700 TL a month (Tr724, 11 May 2019); Istanbul Metropolitan Municipality's 2025 tariff charges 24.500 TL a year for a repeater or small cell on its property, and twice the site selection fee for a cabinet on a BEDAŞ or AYEDAŞ lighting column. Without the agreement, 1.200 TL a pole a year would raise rural OPEX by about half.

    Source: YERKON design choice: a public-sector agreement

    Transport ministry: rules applying the rights-of-way regulation (no per-pole fee) · General Lighting Regulation (no energy from the connection for any purpose but public lighting) · Tr724: advertising on electricity poles (distribution companies have rented pole space since 2015, the smallest poster 4.700 TL a month), 11 May 2019 · Istanbul Metropolitan Municipality, electronic systems, 2025 tariff (yearly site fee for a repeater or small cell)

  2. Roof rent, a year: 24.500 TL/year (published price)

    What the building's owner is paid a year for the roof. No published tariff for a private roof was found; the nearest published fee is the 24.500 TL a year Istanbul Metropolitan Municipality charges for a small radio device. On public buildings the ceiling comes from Law 3194, additional article 9/7: 137.517,20 TL a year in a metropolitan province, 82.510,32 TL elsewhere. Roofs are rented, so the placement search does not offer them; this figure touches no row of the table.

    Source: Istanbul Metropolitan Municipality, electronic systems, 2025 tariff: 24.500 TL a year for a repeater or small cell on its property

    Istanbul Metropolitan Municipality, electronic systems, 2025 tariff (yearly site fee for a repeater or small cell) · Ministry of Agriculture and Forestry: 2025 base station rent rates (site selection fee 27.503,44 TL; cap of Law 3194, Additional Article 9(7))

  3. Cellular data line, a year: 0 TL/year (design choice)

    What a year of data costs an anchor with no line of its own: nothing, because no unit carries a SIM card. A unit on a structure with a line reaches the centre over it: the tunnel's communications spine in a tunnel, a signalised junction's cabinet in town, an ITS point on the road in open country. A unit with no line passes its messages by its own radio to the nearest unit that has one. Each unit sends the centre a short "still working" message at regular intervals, and a unit whose message stops coming is taken as failed. When a receiver sees GPS jamming or spoofing it passes that to a nearby broadcast unit, and the warning reaches the centre the same way.

    Source: YERKON design choice
